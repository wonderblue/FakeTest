# How to Use the Curiosity Vault
### A plain-English guide (no tech skills needed)

Think of this like setting up a **personal newspaper** that only prints the good stuff — chosen by you, with no ads deciding what you see.

You only need to do the setup **once**. After that, reading is easy.

---

## What you have

Two files in this project:

1. **`curiosity_vault.md`** — the menu of recommended reading (who/what/why)
2. **`curiosity_reading_list.opml`** — a shopping list of those sources that an app can import for you automatically

You do **not** need to understand what OPML means. It's just a packing list.

---

## What you need (free)

Pick **one** reading app. Any of these works:

| App | Best if you… | Link |
|-----|--------------|------|
| **Feedly** | Want the easiest option | [feedly.com](https://feedly.com) |
| **Inoreader** | Want more control later | [inoreader.com] |

Also download the **`curiosity_reading_list.opml`** file from this project onto your computer (or phone). Remember where you saved it (Downloads is fine).

## Setup — do this once (about 10 minutes)

### Step 1: Make an account
Go to Feedly (or your chosen app) and sign up with an email. Free is fine.

### Step 2: Import the reading list
1. Open Feedly in a browser
2. Look for something like **Settings → Import / OPML**  
   *(In Feedly: click your profile/menu → **Organize** or **Import OPML**)*
3. Choose the file: `curiosity_reading_list.opml`
4. Click import / confirm

Done. About **30 sources** will appear in your feed automatically.

> If a source fails to import, ignore it. You still have plenty. Some sites prefer email — those are marked in the vault.

### Step 3: Clean it down to 5–7
This is the most important step. **Do not keep everything.**

Scroll through what imported and unfollow / remove anything that doesn't grab you.

**A great starter set (if you're unsure):**
1. Tedium
2. The Public Domain Review
3. Quanta Magazine
4. The Whippet
5. Rest of World
6. Craig Mod *(or The Marginalian if you prefer gentler essays)*

You can always add more later from `curiosity_vault.md`.

### Step 4: Put the app on your phone
Install the Feedly (or NetNewsWire) app. Sign in with the same account. Your list travels with you.

## How to read every day (5–15 minutes)

Open the app when you'd normally scroll social media.

1. You'll see a list of new articles from *your* sources
2. Tap ones that look interesting
3. Read what pulls you in
4. Skip the rest — **guilt-free**

You are not "behind" if you don't read everything. The point is better reading, not more reading.

### The spark-file habit (2 minutes)
Open your Notes app (or any notes place). Make a note called **Spark File**.

Whenever something lights you up, paste:
- the title / link
- one sentence about why it mattered

That's it. Don't organize it.

After a few weeks, re-read the note. You'll start noticing patterns in what *you* care about. That quiet recognition is how curiosity turns into confidence.

---

## Weekly (optional, 10 minutes)

Once a week, ask yourself:

> "What was the most interesting thing I read this week?"

Write one sentence in your spark file. That's enough.

---

## Monthly (optional, 15 minutes)

Open your spark file and ask:

> "What did three unrelated things I read this month have in common?"

Write 3–5 sentences. This is how you start thinking like a generalist — connecting ideas across topics. No expertise required. Just noticing.

## Discovery engines (when you want to wander)

When you feel like exploring *beyond* your list, open one of these in a browser:

| Tool | What to do |
|------|------------|
| **Marginalia Search** — search.marginalia.nu | Type any topic. It finds indie / non-commercial pages Google hides. |
| **Wiby** — wiby.me | Type something. Get old-school, handmade websites. |
| **CalmOp** — calmop.com | Click once. Land somewhere interesting at random. |
| **ooh.directory** — ooh.directory | Browse personal blogs by topic. |

Use these when you're bored and want serendipity — not every day.

## Common worries (answered)

**"I don't have time."**  
Five minutes is enough. One good article beats twenty forgettable posts.

**"I'll fall behind."**  
You can't. These aren't homework. Skip days. The feed waits.

**"I don't understand some of it."**  
Skip to the next one. Curiosity is a buffet, not a test.

**"Is this complicated?"**  
Setup is the only techy part. After that, it's just: open app → tap → read.

**"What if I pick the wrong sources?"**  
You can't. Unfollow what bores you. Add what sparks you. Your list should change over time — that means it's working.

## Your first 7 days (simple plan)

| Day | Do this |
|-----|---------|
| **Day 1** | Set up Feedly + import the OPML file (this guide's setup) |
| **Day 2** | Cull to 5–7 sources |
| **Day 3** | Read one article. Add one line to your Spark File |
| **Day 4** | Open Marginalia Search. Search something you're curious about |
| **Day 5** | Read whatever looks interesting in your feed |
| **Day 6** | Rest or skim — no pressure |
| **Day 7** | Look at your Spark File. Notice what themes showed up |

After a week you'll already feel the difference: less noise, more wonder.

**Import the list → keep only what sparks you → read a little each day → save what lights you up.**
