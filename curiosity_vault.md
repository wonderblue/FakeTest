# The Curiosity Vault

> A hand-picked collection of under-the-radar, mind-stimulating things to read —
> in the spirit of [kottke.org](https://kottke.org): eclectic, intelligent, and
> built for serendipity. Curated for a curious generalist who wants to read more,
> think wider, and quietly build confidence.

**How this is organized:** by the *kind of curiosity itch* each source scratches —
not alphabetically. Skim the moods, follow what sparks you, ignore the rest.
There's no "finishing" this. That's the point.

A companion file, [`curiosity_reading_list.opml`](./curiosity_reading_list.opml),
lets you import every feed below into an RSS reader in one click (see
[How To Use](#how-to-use-this) at the bottom).

## How To Use This

You'll get the most out of this if you stop *chasing tabs* and build a small,
calm system. Here's the whole thing in four steps:

### 1. Pick 5–7 that made you go "ooh"
Don't subscribe to everything — that's how you burn out. Start small. My
suggested starter set for a kottke fan:

- **Tedium** (weird-obscure deep dives)
- **The Public Domain Review** (beautiful-historical)
- **Quanta Magazine** (wonder / science)
- **The Whippet** (curiosity newsletter)
- **Gwern** (rabbit holes)
- **Marginalia Search** (for hunting your own)

### 2. Put them in one place (RSS)
An RSS reader gives you a single, chronological, **algorithm-free** feed of only
the sources *you* chose. No one deciding what you see. All of these are free:

- **Feedly** — easiest, works everywhere (web + phone)
- **Inoreader** — more powerful, great filters
- **NetNewsWire** — beautiful, free, Apple devices

**One-click import:** In any reader, look for *Import* / *Import OPML* and select
[`curiosity_reading_list.opml`](./curiosity_reading_list.opml) from this repo.
Every feed below loads at once. Then delete the ones that don't spark joy.

> Note: a handful of sources are email-first (marked ✉️) — for those, subscribe
> on their website. If any RSS feed in the OPML fails to load, the site almost
> certainly has a "Subscribe / RSS" link; most modern blogs use `/feed`,
> `/feed/`, `/rss`, or `/rss.xml` at the end of the URL.

### 3. Keep a "spark file"
Open one running note (Notes app, a doc, anywhere). Each week, paste the 2–3
things that genuinely lit you up — a sentence, a link, an idea. Don't organize
it. Just collect.

After a month, read it back. You'll start seeing **your own taste** emerge —
the themes you keep returning to. That recognition ("oh, *this* is what I care
about") is the quiet engine of confidence. You stop being a consumer and become
someone with a point of view.

### 4. Once a month, connect the dots
Ask yourself one question: *"What did three unrelated things I read this month
secretly have in common?"* That's the horizontal-reading muscle — the same skill
that lets a researcher walk into any field and hold their own. You already have
the instinct. This just makes it a habit.
